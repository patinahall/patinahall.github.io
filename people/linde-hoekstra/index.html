Personal profile

Linde Hoekstra

Linde moves between dance studios and walking trails with a small notebook close at hand. It fills with overheard phrases and places worth returning to: fragments that may seem unrelated until rhythm, context or a repeated detail draws a line between them.

  • Dance
  • Walking trails

A way of noticing

Notice first. Name the pattern second.

Dance gives Linde a feel for rhythm before it has to be explained. Walking and writing slow the same instinct down: a phrase, place or repeated shape earns a line in the notebook before she decides what it might belong with.

Dance, trails & a notebook

Collecting fragments without flattening them

Dance is where Linde experiences structure through movement. A repeated phrase can return with a different weight, and a small change in timing can make the whole sequence feel new. She likes the discipline of repetition without sameness.

On long walks, a pocket-sized notebook becomes an archive of places and language. Linde records an overheard phrase or a doorway worth finding again. The value lies in the fragment and the attention it awakened.

The vintage eye

Art Deco after the curtain rises

Art Deco appeals to Linde because geometry never quite suppresses performance. A sharp line can still feel theatrical; symmetry can frame an entrance; gold, black and saturated colour can turn an ordinary announcement into an event.

Old theatre posters bring that world closest. They preserve a date, a typeface and a promise made to an audience that has long since gone home. Linde enjoys the way a single sheet can hold design, place and anticipation together.

At PatinaHall

Helping people find what they cannot yet name

As Search & Data Engineer, Linde works with catalogues that do not share one vocabulary. A seller may say low cabinet, credenza, sideboard or dressoir; another may begin with a maker, material or decade. Her task is to help those descriptions meet without rewriting every store in the same voice.

The notebook instinct matters here. PatinaHall should support precise searches and still reward someone who begins with a room, a material or a half-remembered shape. The best result is not always the most famous object; sometimes it is the piece a buyer could not yet name.

About this profile
