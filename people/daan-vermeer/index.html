Personal profile

Daan Vermeer

A chance trip with friends put Daan on his first climbing route. He stayed for the unusual lines—the ones that make him pause, study the surface and find a way through that is not immediately obvious. Away from the wall, he photographs old buildings and looks for the construction details time has left visible.

  • Climbing
  • Architectural photography
  • 1920s–30s design

A way of looking

The most interesting route is rarely the most obvious one.

Climbing taught Daan to read a surface before moving: where the weight can settle, which hold changes the route and when a pause reveals an option that speed would miss. Photographing old buildings asks for the same patience, only with light, structure and traces of use instead of stone.

Climbing & photography

Routes, façades and the pleasure of looking twice

Daan began climbing after joining friends on an unplanned trip. What could have remained a one-off afternoon became a habit of seeking unusual routes: the lines that reward balance, observation and a willingness to reconsider the first solution.

His camera comes out for old buildings rather than polished new façades. A repaired window, worn stair or unexpected junction between two materials can tell a longer story than a perfectly centred elevation. The photograph is a way to keep that evidence without tidying it away.

The vintage eye

A mechanical world in miniature

Old mechanical watches appeal to Daan because a small case can hold an entire legible system. Gears, springs and hands cooperate quietly, and a good repair respects both the mechanism and the evidence of the life around it.

He is especially drawn to the 1920s and 1930s, when geometry, industrial confidence and careful ornament often met in the same object. The attraction is not nostalgia for a flawless past; it is the clarity with which construction and style can still speak to one another.

At PatinaHall

Making complicated systems easier to read

As Lead Software Engineer, Daan looks across the routes that connect seller tools, catalogue preparation, public discovery and the handoff back to an independent store. His job is to decide which ideas belong together, which must stay separate and how change can move without surprising everything around it.

The same instinct that studies a climbing wall or a watch movement keeps PatinaHall's architecture calm and repairable. Buyers should see a clear catalogue, sellers should understand what the system is doing and unusual objects should never be forced into false precision merely to make the software look neat.
