Roadmap

What's next: furniture stories on listings, and alerts for pieces you want

Two committed pieces of work. Listings will link to the object history and buying guide that apply to them, and you will be able to ask to be told when a piece you want appears.

Published 30 October 2026

Committed: linking listings to the history and guide that apply

Open a listing for a Gispen 412 and you get photographs, a price, measurements and the shop. You do not get the Gispen 412 story — how that tubular outline carries upholstery, leaves the space open and gives a little as you sit — although it is written and on this site in three languages. Read the story, and nothing tells you that one is for sale.

The gap runs both ways. A chrome-framed chair in the catalogue has a guide about exactly what tarnish, pitting and plating loss can and cannot tell you, and the listing never mentions it.

So: a listing will carry the object's history where we have researched it, and the buying guide that matches what you would actually be inspecting. A story will say when one is for sale.

Committed: alerts for a piece you want

Retail assumes there is another one. Vintage does not. Arriving on the wrong week means the piece you wanted is simply not here, and there is no way to ask us to watch for it.

So: tell us what you are looking for, and we will tell you when one appears. A standing request rather than a search you have to remember to repeat. Sellers get the same thing from the other side — what people came looking for and did not find.

Being explored, not yet committed

Narrowing the catalogue by size, material and price rather than browsing a category. Delivery shown as a fact on the listing instead of only as the subject of a guide. And condition described the same way on every piece: what the shop observed, and what remains unknown.

These three are not scheduled. They are listed here so you can tell them apart from the two above, which are.
