Library update

A Viking toolbox, Darwin’s chair and eight more stories

Ten new Furniture Library stories follow ingenious objects and the people behind them, from milk-protein inlay to a cupboard that comes out to play.

Published 10 September 2026

Darwin’s writing chair acquired supports from a bedframe. Mackintosh’s yellow cabinet inlay began with milk protein. A Viking chest held tools for both wood and metal. Our ten latest Furniture Library stories start with details like these, then follow them into the lives of makers and users.

The articles are live now, with shorter openings, clearer chapters and illustrations placed beside the things they explain. You can begin with a familiar name or an object you have never seen before.

Furniture that does something unexpected

Oeben’s mechanical table sends its decorated top backwards just as a fitted drawer moves towards the sitter. Closing it winds the springs for the next opening. Darwin’s chair offers a quieter kind of ingenuity: wheels, added height and a writing board helped it accommodate one person’s working habits.

Elsewhere, Pratone asks you to find a seat among oversized blades of soft grass. Alma Siedhoff-Buscher’s play cupboard gives children movable pieces to sit on and play with. Tejo Remy starts with mismatched old drawers and builds a new home around each one.

The people and work behind the shape

Clara Porset’s story follows pine and woven fibres into a furnishing proposal for farming families in Mexico, including the collaboration behind it. Lilly Reich shows how curtains can organise an exhibition café. Thomas Day’s workshop brings distinctive furniture together with a harder history of unequal freedoms and enslaved labour.

The Mästermyr chest takes us back to the tools themselves. A new illustrated guide to four tool types shows how cutting wood, holding metal and shaping it belonged to one portable working collection. Mackintosh’s cabinet offers another material surprise: the delicate yellow inlay is casein, made from milk protein.

Choose one detail and follow it

Each story includes sources and a clear account of the pictures. Historical images, explanatory diagrams and editorial reconstructions are identified in their captions. Open the toolbox or take a closer look at Darwin’s chair, then follow your curiosity through the Library.
