New Guides

Five new Guides, and a plain account of how paying works

Joints, lamps, picture frames, curtains and furniture that has to move again — five new Guides, published in three languages at once. Alongside them, how money is meant to move between a shop and a buyer.

Published 30 October 2026

Five questions that come up once the piece is in the room

Most of what PatinaHall writes answers a question asked in front of a listing. These five are asked slightly later — once the piece is yours, or once you are standing in a flat you do not own.

What a visible furniture joint can tell you keeps three things apart that are easy to confuse: how a piece was built, what condition it is in, and whether it can be taken apart to get up the stairs. It also separates a peg that is part of the joint itself from a separate dowel, which is a different claim about how the thing was made.

Several vintage lamps, one comfortable room gives each old lamp a job instead of a position, and builds an evening plan around the people actually in the room. An old frame for a picture you already love measures the opening, the rebate behind it and the usable depth before anybody promises the picture will fit. Vintage curtains that fit your window compares a second-hand pair against your own track or pole — finished width, drop, heading, lining, and what an alteration can realistically rescue.

And Vintage furniture for this rental and the next move is for the situation half of Europe is in: choosing pieces you can live with now, while writing down how they came in, what the largest single part is, and what moving them again will actually involve.

Fifteen existing Guides went deeper

The same release expanded fifteen Guides that were already published — among them measuring a table for a room, measuring furniture for the route into your home, measuring a vintage bed for a modern mattress, telling teak from walnut and rosewood, reading solid wood against veneer and laminate, judging restored or refinished pieces, and arranging professional delivery.

All five new Guides were published in English, Dutch and German on the same day. That is now the default rather than a follow-up project.

How paying is meant to work

We have also published a plain account of the money: what a shop's payment account is and why it belongs to the shop rather than to us, the order of events from a buyer's first message through the agreed piece and the agreed delivery price to the payment itself, and a worked example with real numbers — the piece, the delivery, what the buyer pays, what comes off it and what reaches the shop's own balance.

To be clear about what this is: it is the explanation, not the feature. PatinaHall does not take payment for a piece yet. We wrote the account first on purpose, because the questions people ask before paying a stranger for an old cabinet deserve an answer that exists in one place and does not change depending on who is replying.
