Five new guides to choosing and living with vintage furniture

Visible joints, several lamps in one room, an old picture frame, second-hand curtains, and furnishing a rental you will move out of. Published in English, Dutch and German.

Published 30 October 2026

Five new guides

Most PatinaHall guides answer a question you ask in front of a listing. These five are asked slightly later — once the piece is yours, or once you are standing in a flat you do not own. All five are published in English, Dutch and German, and are in the Guides hub under their topics.

What a visible furniture joint can tell you — keeps three things apart that are easy to confuse: how a piece was built, what condition it is in, and whether it can be taken apart to get up the stairs.

Several vintage lamps, one comfortable room — gives each old lamp a job rather than a position, and builds an evening plan around the people using the room.

An old frame for a picture you already love — measures the visible opening, the rebate behind it and the usable depth before anyone promises the picture will fit.

Vintage curtains that fit your window — compares a second-hand pair against your own track or pole: finished width, drop, heading, lining, and what an alteration can realistically rescue.

Vintage furniture for this rental and the next move — choosing pieces you can live with now while recording how they came in, what the largest single part is, and what moving them again will involve.

Fifteen existing guides went deeper

The same release expanded fifteen guides that were already published — among them measuring a table for a room, measuring furniture for the route into your home, measuring a vintage bed for a modern mattress, telling teak from walnut and rosewood, reading solid wood against veneer and laminate, judging restored or refinished pieces, and arranging professional delivery.
