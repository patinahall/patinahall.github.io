Guides & Library

New Guides, familiar furniture and a few good stories

Practical help for a table that extends, the story behind a familiar sofa, and a trolley with swinging trays: there is more to read in PatinaHall’s Guides and Furniture Library.

Published 7 September 2026

A table has caught your eye. The price seems possible, the shape feels right, and then you notice the extra leaves stacked against the wall. Do they all fit? Where will they live between dinners? There is a Guide for that.

We have expanded PatinaHall’s Guides and Furniture Library with more practical questions, more everyday furniture histories and more photographs that help explain what you are looking at. We have also revisited existing articles to improve the writing, check the facts and replace weak visual examples.

Useful beside a listing

The new extending-table Guide starts with the table you will use most days, then follows the leaves, supports, opening sequence and occupied room. A short seller video can answer a question that two polished photographs leave untouched.

Other recent Guides cover a wobbly wooden chair, a sagging sofa, folding chairs and glass display cabinets. Each turns a familiar uncertainty into something useful to inspect, measure or ask. You should leave knowing what to do next.

The interesting part of an ordinary object

The Library is spending more time with furniture you might recognise from a friend’s flat or a second-hand listing. Our KLIPPAN story follows the removable cover: a simple decision that brings washing, changing tastes and another household into the life of a sofa.

Joe Colombo’s Boby trolley offers a different small pleasure. Its shallow trays swing out around a pivot, letting the contents come to your hand from the side. Follow that movement and a compact storage unit opens onto a bigger idea about a workspace that can travel with its owner.

There are also stories about BILLY bookcases, G Plan tables, folding chairs and modular storage. Familiarity is a good starting point for curiosity. You do not need to be collecting museum pieces to enjoy furniture history.

More to look at along the way

Recent everyday-furniture stories now have more photographs showing the whole object, with details placed beside the passages they explain. Useful diagrams remain for movement, construction and room planning. Captions identify the example and credit the image, so you can tell what you are seeing.

Start with a practical question or a familiar name. Read the extending-table Guide before your next seller message, or meet Boby and see whether you can look at an ordinary drawer quite the same way again.
